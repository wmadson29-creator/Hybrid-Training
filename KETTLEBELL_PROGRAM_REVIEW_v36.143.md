Prepared October 2, 2026, America/Phoenix. The user asked whether the KB days were too easy for full-body conditioning and muscle building, then clarified that the concern was exercise count, sets, and reps rather than starting weights. This is a review of the existing v36.141 program. v36.143 leaves its prescriptions and progression unchanged.

## Assessment

My assessment is that the KB resistance dose is a reasonable starting addition to this particular week. The conditioning dose is conservative. These are different judgments: four compound exercises can supply meaningful resistance work, while brief swing blocks still depend on the open days for broader aerobic and quality conditioning.

The exact A/B/C recipe is an app adaptation. It has not been established as optimal in a trial, and the number of exercises alone cannot establish adequacy for this user. More volume can be appropriate when results and recovery support it; adding exercises simply to make each day look larger does not resolve that question.

Starting KB totals are **eight press sets, seven squat sets, seven row sets, and 170 swings across three days**. The press total contains six overhead sets and two horizontal sets. A unilateral row set is credited to each trained side; it does not double that muscle's weekly set count. These totals describe exercise patterns and cannot be treated as the same dose to every involved muscle. Swing rounds also cannot be counted as equivalent hypertrophy sets for every posterior-chain muscle.

Monday and Thursday already add Barbell squatting, pulling, and pressing. Wednesday and Saturday remain open opportunities. The KB presets were sized around those anchors: B has two sets per resistance movement; C reduces squat and swing volume before Monday. The builder estimates approximately 29, 28, and 29 minutes for the default paired-bell plans. A shorter session is not automatically an ineffective one.

## Why the conditioning concern has merit

The initial 50/80/40-swing blocks include 60/60/75-second rests. They are bounded ballistic supplements. They do not demonstrate sufficient endurance development by themselves, and feeling very little cardiorespiratory challenge would be useful feedback about this component. The open-day run, swim, bike, and quality conditioning work remains part of the intended regimen.

The strength sets should also provide a meaningful effort within their prescribed ranges. The program's existing progression compares completed exposures: two comfortable full results can earn reps, then the next available bell; swings earn rounds before a bell change. This review focuses on the dose and exercise structure rather than asserting that untested default weights match the user.

## Decision for this release

Retain the current KB A/B/C baseline, rest, rep ranges, and progression. Keep explicit custom routines and historical rows intact. The supplied export has no completed KB exposures, so there is no direct evidence that this user's completed KB sets are consistently trivial or that additional baseline volume is tolerated across the whole week.

Future adjustment should distinguish what is lacking. If muscle/rep performance stalls despite good completion and recovery, review direct weekly work for the affected muscles and add a bounded dose there. If strength improves but conditioning remains insufficient, review swing work/rest and the open-day conditioning dose. Neither finding requires unrelated extra exercises on every KB day. No new within-workout automatic autoregulation or new weekly optimizer was introduced.
