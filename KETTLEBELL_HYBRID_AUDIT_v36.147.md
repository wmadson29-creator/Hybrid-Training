# Kettlebell hybrid plan: deeper evidence and whole-week audit — v36.147

Prepared October 4, 2026, America/Phoenix. Reviewed for the user's goals of full-body muscle building and conditioning, with two fixed Barbell days, three fixed kettlebell slots, and two conditioning/open days. This is a targeted evidence review and an audit of the actual app, not a systematic review or a trial of this exact program.

## Decision

The previous research supported the general approach but did not adequately audit the combined week's muscle coverage or distinguish swing rounds from controlled resistance sets. The clearest gap was controlled hamstring loading after removing Barbell Deadlift from Hybrid. More chest, rowing, and squat work was not the first priority.

The revised plan adds two Double RDL sets on A and B and removes one press and one row set from Sunday C. Weekly KB resistance work rises from 22 to 24 sets, while all 170 baseline swings and their rests stay unchanged. The app also corrects swing stimulus attribution, floor-press direction, and the RDL abbreviation's anatomy. These are reasoned starting choices. No study identifies these exact sets, reps, rests, or model coefficients as optimal for this user.

## What was examined

- Current `getTBPlan`, the A/B/C presets, available bell settings, exact-slot progression, full-secondary construction, fit-to-time behavior, and the muscle/fatigue/fitness calculation paths.
- General resistance-training volume, direct versus indirect work, effort, rest, purposeful variation, adjacent-day recovery, and concurrent training.
- Kettlebell-specific training trials and limitations, including distinct aerobic results under different protocols.
- Original publicly available descriptions of Dry Fighting Weight, The Giant, and MAXIMORUM. Paid manuals and complete progression tables were not obtained or reproduced. Coaching descriptions and testimonials were not treated as comparative trials.

The supplied export has 165 workout rows but no completed KB rows or saved custom KB routines. It cannot establish the user's current KB rep maximum, training response, or tolerance of the proposed dose. Synthetic QA histories below are software fixtures, not user workouts.

The hamstring finding is supported by a small MRI squat-training trial in which hamstring volume did not increase [4], and by the IUSCA discussion of exercise selection [3]. It does not prove that swings are ineffective or that this exact RDL dose is best.

## Current prescription

Finish an exercise's straight sets before moving on. Use controlled resistance work with about 2–3 reps in reserve; keep swings explosive and stop a round when speed or form deteriorates. The prescribed rest is between completed sets; finish both sides of unilateral work first.

Baseline paired-bell plans estimate approximately A 33, B 32, and C 25 minutes, including a five-minute allowance and exercise transitions. Previous estimates were 29, 28, and 30. These are app estimates, not measured user durations. Earned reps and rounds, single-bell substitutions, longer rests, and warm-ups can change actual time. Short budgets reduce sets while retaining the complete exercise list; a budget too small to fit that minimum declines the full build. A/B have five exercises and C four.

RDL instructions: soften the knees, move the hips back, lower under control to a range limited by hamstring length and stable back position, then stand through the hips. This is a controlled hinge, not a swing. A one-bell setup uses Single-Leg RDL at its own supported bell setting, with reps on each side; it does not inherit a pair's total weight. Added RDLs use the same established rep-first progression, separately for A and B.

Four controlled hinge sets are a modest initial addition. They are not a promise to maximize hamstring growth. There is still no dedicated knee-flexion hamstring exercise, and RDLs do not replace every function or region trained by a leg curl/Nordic variation. Calves and arm isolation are not comprehensive physique priorities in this minimalist hybrid. These are explicit limits to revisit if muscle growth or specific regional development stalls; they do not justify adding many new exercises simultaneously.

## Evidence, access, and application

The review used original journal/publisher pages, the professional position stand, the author's institutional thesis record, and original coaching pages. Access restrictions and short studies limit certainty. No exercise was chosen solely from an EMG ranking, and improvements in body composition or performance were not relabeled as direct evidence of muscle hypertrophy.

| Source | Material actually reviewed | What it supports and does not establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [1] ACSM 2026 overview of reviews | Official summary, indexed abstract and available indexed sections; complete journal text was not obtained. | Consistent goal-specific resistance work, weekly volume and useful effort without mandatory failure. Broad guidance is not an exact KB dose or a requirement for ten additional sets of every muscle. |
| [2] Pelland et al., 2026 volume/frequency meta-regressions | Published abstract/preview; full published article behind access restrictions. | Direct/indirect distinctions, increasing returns that diminish, and different volume/frequency relationships for strength and hypertrophy. | [4] Kubo et al., 2019 | Published abstract, MRI outcomes. | In a small ten-week squat trial, hamstring volume did not increase. Do not infer hamstring development solely from squat sets. Not a direct comparison of RDLs with kettlebell swings. |
| [5] Robinson et al., 2024 | Published abstract. | Closer proximity to failure was associated with greater hypertrophy in exploratory meta-regression; strength was less dependent on it. Protocol/reporting inconsistencies and population/outcome differences make them poor justification for this exact prescription. They are not used as central support for the changes.

## Model correction and its limits

Previously, an eight-round, ten-rep Two-Hand Swing row at RPE 7 received about 6.29 effective hamstring sets, 5.82 hypertrophy-stimulus units, 6.62 acute-fatigue units, and zero conditioning load. The swing was classified as a hinge but missed the KB power flag and the work-capacity path used by other ballistics. That is a software attribution finding, not a physiological measurement.

Now recognized catalogued swings receive one-quarter of the ordinary effective-set/hypertrophy credit and one-half of ordinary strength credit; original acute fatigue is retained. The same fixture has about 1.57 hamstring set-credit, 1.46 hypertrophy units, and 6.62 fatigue units. These coefficients are deliberately conservative app heuristics, **not empirically validated equivalences**. They distinguish short crisp ballistic rounds from controlled near-failure sets without assuming swings have zero muscle/strength value.

Completed swing set details determine rounds and reps when present. Estimated active work uses two seconds per swing; rest uses logged positive set-rest values, otherwise planned rest, otherwise an explicitly marked 60-second assumption. Work-capacity load scales with that work, effort, and a bounded density factor. Estimates are flagged. Total mixed-workout duration is not assigned to each swing exercise. Swings gain work-capacity and power attribution, but no fabricated running/swimming/cycling efficiency or direct aerobic-base adaptation. No VO2max is inferred.

KB floor presses now map to chest/horizontal pressing rather than the generic overhead-press family. `Double RDL` and `Single-Leg RDL` now resolve the `RDL` abbreviation into the hip-extensor hamstring and controlled-eccentric paths. These model changes apply when history is interpreted; saved rows themselves are not rewritten. Skipped rows earn no new muscle, fatigue, conditioning or power credit.

## Progression, recovery and next calibration

The established progression requires two comparable complete full workouts for the exact exercise and KB slot. Add one rep within the range, then the next supported bell and reset reps. A's RDL result does not advance B's RDL. Short add-ons, old different exercises, and incomplete/poor-quality work do not earn full-dose progression. Recovery caution holds an earned increase; manual loads and saved custom workouts remain authoritative.

Sunday remains a full-body workout, with a smaller overlap dose. It is not a guarantee of full recovery by Monday or an instruction to train through poor quality. Actual class muscles, conditioning effort, soreness, completed doses, and nearby anchors still inform the app. The open-day model version is refreshed so old generated advice is reconsidered. Explicit commitments, completed workouts and custom choices remain preserved.

Evaluate after several comparable exposures using completed sets/reps, the actual bell setting, RPE/technique, and recovery into Monday/Thursday. First establish whether the resistance sets meet the stated effort and whether progress is repeatable. If work is consistently too easy, earn reps/bell increases before indiscriminately adding volume. If posterior-chain progress stalls while recovery is good, reassess hinge dose or knee-flexion coverage. Conditioning progress needs actual modality-specific work and results; 170 swings alone do not establish a sufficient aerobic plan. No new automatic weekly set optimizer or within-workout load-adjustment feature is implied by this release.
